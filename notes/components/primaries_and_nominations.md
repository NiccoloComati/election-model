### 2023-09-18 · [Is a Trump-Biden rematch inevitable?](https://www.natesilver.net/p/is-a-trump-biden-rematch-inevitable)
**empirical regularity** · FiveThirtyEight Silver era primary analysis · explicit

A vintage FiveThirtyEight (Silver-era) analysis shows a sigmoid relationship between a candidate's early national primary polling average (January-June of the year before the election) and the chance of winning the nomination. A candidate at 50% or higher has a 90%+ chance. Reading the chart, about 15% maps to roughly 10%, about 30% to roughly 45%, about 40% to roughly 75%, and 60% to roughly 97%.

*Numbers:* polling threshold = 50 percent (early national primary polling average); nomination probability at threshold = 90 or higher percent (empirical from FiveThirtyEight chart); approx curve points = 15%→~10%, 25%→~30%, 30%→~45%, 40%→~75%, 60%→~97% percent (read from chart)

*Why:* Uses historical data on how early primary leaders fared.

> a candidate polling at 50 percent or higher in national primary polls is empirically in a very strong position, equating to a 90 percent or higher chance of being his party’s nominee.

### 2024-08-24 · [We removed RFK Jr. from our model. But it didn’t hurt Kamala.](https://www.natesilver.net/p/we-removed-rfk-jr-from-our-model)

### 2025-02-17 · [So, how did the polls do in 2024? It’s complicated](https://www.natesilver.net/p/so-how-did-the-polls-do-in-2024-its)
**empirical regularity** · general principle · explicit

Presidential primary polls have by far the largest error of any race type, for two reasons. First, multicandidate fields mean voters who like several options switch late or vote tactically for candidates who seem likely to finish strongly. Second, low turnout makes it hard to predict who will vote.

*Numbers:* primary error all years = 9.1 points

*Why:* Multiple candidates and low turnout.

> presidential primaries usually feature multiple candidates... Second, presidential primaries feature much lower turnout than general elections.

### 2026-03-23 · [SBSQ #30: Will liberals turn against sports betting?](https://www.natesilver.net/p/sbsq-30-will-liberals-turn-against)
**interpretation guidance** · Silver analysis, 2026 · explicit

In reading multi-candidate primary polls, Silver allocates undecided voters according to the state's expected partisanship and assumes some support for minor candidates of a party will migrate to that party's leaders, because voters avoid wasted votes and minor candidates often drop out and endorse. In California's March 2026 governor polling, adding the 13 percent undecided to Democrats turns a 15-point combined Democratic lead into 28 points, matching the state benchmark of D+28.8.

*Numbers:* California benchmark = D+28.8 points (Silver Bulletin benchmark, March 2026); combined Democratic share = 50.1 percent (CA governor polling average); combined Republican share = 34.9 percent (CA governor polling average); undecided = 13 percent (CA governor polling average)

*Why:* Undecideds in a deep-blue state are mostly Democrats; strategic voters consolidate behind viable candidates.

> I think it’s pretty safe to assume that most of that undecided vote will flow to Democrats.
