### 2023-09-18 · [Is a Trump-Biden rematch inevitable?](https://www.natesilver.net/p/is-a-trump-biden-rematch-inevitable)
**method** · general principle · explicit

To derive the market-implied probability of a joint outcome (a Biden-Trump rematch), Silver multiplies the two separate nomination probabilities, assuming the events are independent: 65% x 67% = 44%.

*Numbers:* Biden market nomination probability = 64.6 percent (ElectionBettingOdds.com, Sept 18 2023 (chart); rounded to 65% in text); Trump market nomination probability = 66.8 percent (ElectionBettingOdds.com, Sept 18 2023 (chart); rounded to 67%); implied rematch probability = 44 percent (product under independence)

*Why:* In a footnote he says he can't think of a good first-order reason to think the events are correlated.

> Assuming that Biden and Trump’s odds are independent from one another, that means the chance of a rematch is only 65% x 67% = 44%.

### 2023-09-18 · [Is a Trump-Biden rematch inevitable?](https://www.natesilver.net/p/is-a-trump-biden-rematch-inevitable)
**method** · general principle · explicit

Silver checks market prices for internal consistency. If markets were pricing large legal risk to Trump's nomination, that risk should also lower his general-election odds, both because a conviction is more likely by November and because swing voters care more. Instead, markets imply a 45% general-election win chance conditional on nomination (29.6/66.8), which Silver calls high for a challenger facing an incumbent.

*Numbers:* Trump presidency market probability = 29.6 percent (ElectionBettingOdds chart); conditional general election win = 45 percent (implied by markets)

*Why:* Coherence between linked markets reveals whether a risk is actually priced.

> these markets imply a 45 percent chance of Trump winning the general election conditional upon being nominated, which is pretty high for someone running against an incumbent.
