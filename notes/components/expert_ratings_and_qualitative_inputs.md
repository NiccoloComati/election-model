### 2024-07-05 · [The broken leg problem](https://www.natesilver.net/p/the-broken-leg-problem)
**design principle** · general principle · explicit

Silver endorses Meehl's 'broken leg' heuristic: override or discount a model's output when there is a material factor outside its training data. The heuristic should be used sparingly, only when the extenuating circumstance is obvious enough that any reasonable nonpartisan observer would agree. Forecasters usually err in the opposite direction, through too much special pleading.

*Why:* Real life is messy, but subjective overrides usually do more harm than good.

> You should reserve it for cases where it's bloody obvious there's some extenuating circumstance that any reasonable nonpartisan observer could agree upon.

### 2024-10-10 · [Why you should mostly ignore "internal polls"](https://www.natesilver.net/p/why-you-should-mostly-ignore-internal)
**design principle** · general principle · explicit

Forecasters should prefer the unbiased outside view that public polls and models provide over insider reports, rumors and conventional wisdom about internal polls. Information fidelity degrades along a telephone chain: journalists, then well-connected elites, then randos on Twitter.

*Why:* Insider conventional wisdom can become detached from reality through feedback loops, as with the 2022 red wave and the 2016 belief that Clinton couldn't lose.

> So long the data, and short the vibes.

**design principle** · general principle · explicit

Gut instinct and media 'vibes' should not override models or other relatively objective indicators. Elections are too infrequent to calibrate intuition, and guts tend to extrapolate the last election or echo the media.

*Why:* Gut can be calibrated in poker over thousands of hands, but elections occur once every four years.

> Basically, you should stick to the models or other relatively objective indicators.

> They won't get you from 50 percent to 60 percent, or even 55 percent, but maybe they can get you to 52 or 53 if they mostly point in the same direction.

### 2026-08-11 · [How FLIPR forecasts the midterm elections](https://www.natesilver.net/p/flipr-midterms-model-methodology)
**method** · FLIPR 2026 · explicit

Step 4 (Deluxe): (1) translate ratings from Cook Political Report, Inside Elections and Sabato's Crystal Ball into implied margins, using a Bayesian treatment for Solid/Safe ratings, which are informative only when Classic puts the race near a category boundary; (2) estimate each outlet's implied national environment; (3) shift the ratings to FLIPR's national environment to de-bias them; (4) blend with Classic.

*Why:* Experts' value is in rank-ordering races, not in the national environment.

> De-bias the expert ratings by shifting them to FLIPR’s estimate of the national environment.

### 2026-09-20 · [Expert ratings are ignoring signs of a blue wave](https://www.natesilver.net/p/expert-ratings-are-ignoring-signs)
**method** · FLIPR 2026 · explicit

'Solid' ratings are a censored category, since a Solid race may be 15 or 60 points. FLIPR uses Classic's forecast to impute an expert margin and applies a Bayesian check: if it is implausible that the race would be rated anything but Solid (e.g. a Massachusetts Senate race), the rating has essentially no effect; if the race is plausibly competitive but rated Solid (e.g. a Minnesota race with lean about D+5), it can have a fairly big impact.

*Numbers:* Minnesota neutral-environment lean = about D+5 points (example)

*Why:* Without this, Deluxe projections would become too compressed.

> For Solid races, FLIPR basically relies on Classic (polls + fundamentals) to impute an expert margin.

### 2026-09-20 · [Expert ratings are ignoring signs of a blue wave](https://www.natesilver.net/p/expert-ratings-are-ignoring-signs)
**method** · FLIPR 2026 · explicit

FLIPR de-biases expert ratings by shifting them to match FLIPR Classic's national environment; for example, an all-'toss-up' Michigan became D+5.7 after de-biasing. The adjustment is dynamic and shrinks as experts move their ratings toward the objective environment late in the cycle.

*Why:* Experts' value lies in intangibles and private information, not their view of the national environment.

> So FLIPR debiases the expert ratings so that they match FLIPR Classic’s estimate of the national political environment.
