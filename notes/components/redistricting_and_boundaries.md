### 2026-08-22 · [A tour of the 48 key House races](https://www.natesilver.net/p/a-tour-of-the-48-key-house-races)
**method** · FLIPR 2026 · explicit

For redistricted seats, FLIPR measures how much the new and old districts overlap to calibrate the remaining incumbency advantage, and it still treats incumbency as meaningful in such cases, more so than some other models. Example: about 60 percent of FL-9's residents carried over, so Democrat Darren Soto keeps some incumbency credit in an R+18 redrawn seat, at 38 percent to win.

*Numbers:* FL-9 population overlap = about 60 percent (old vs new district); Soto win prob = 38 percent (Aug 2026)

> It evaluates exactly how much of the new and old districts overlap to calibrate its estimate of the remaining incumbency advantage

### 2026-08-22 · [A tour of the 48 key House races](https://www.natesilver.net/p/a-tour-of-the-48-key-house-races)
**parameter or number** · FLIPR 2026 · explicit

Democratic incumbents in seats redrawn to roughly R+10 to R+12 (TX-34, NC-1, FL-22) retained 63 to 68 percent win probabilities in FLIPR, largely due to incumbency plus the D+7 environment. Marcy Kaptur in a redrawn R+11 OH-9 was at about 63 percent.

*Numbers:* redrawn seat lean = R+10 to R+12 points (TX-34, NC-1, FL-22); D hold probability = 63-68 percent (Aug 2026)

> They would all have been roughly R +10 to R +12 in 2024 under their states’ new maps
